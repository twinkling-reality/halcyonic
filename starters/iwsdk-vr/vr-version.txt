Make a VR version of the web app in {original}.
Read that folder to understand the app: what you see, what you do, and whether it is a game.
Do not change anything there.
Build it in this project with the Immersive Web SDK, so it works in VR with controllers or hands and on a laptop with a mouse and keyboard.
If it is a game, build its main play:
start from the whole small game at the end of this project's AGENTS.md and change it into this one, importing from src/kit.ts.
Keep to the main play and its score: leave out title, end and pause screens, menus and best scores.
Set it in the world from src/worlds.ts that fits the original, and keep its feel.
If it is not a game, first find from its own code (its routes, its main screen, what its data is) the one thing people open it to do, write in a line or two what it is for and what of it this VR version will carry, then build without waiting.
Build that use, working, with the app's own content: what a person sees and what they do with their hands. Its landing or marketing page is not its use, unless the app is nothing else.
What cannot come with it (a server, other people, an account), carry with the app's own sample content, or where it has none with a small stand-in of the same kind, and say so; never drop it silently.
If it is not a game, let its own look (its colours, pictures and effects) dress that use, never replace it; when its use is itself a view (a sky, a map, a model), bring that view as 'A view from another app' in this project's AGENTS.md says.
Read every input through src/play-input.ts.
Copy pictures and sounds you need from the original into public/ and name them in src/assets.ts.
Put the app's name and one line on what to do on its welcome card.
Run npx tsc --noEmit and fix every error it reports before you finish.
End your turn with a few lines for the person: what the app is for, what this VR version carries, what stands in for what could not come, and what it leaves out and why.
