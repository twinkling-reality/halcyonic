Make a VR version of the web app in {original}.
Read that folder to understand the app: what you see, what you do, and whether it is a game.
Do not change anything there.
Do not put your plan to the person to confirm: build it, and ask only where these words leave a real choice.
Build it in this project with the Immersive Web SDK, so it works in VR with controllers or hands and on a laptop with a mouse and keyboard.
If it is a game, build its main play:
start from the whole small game at the end of this project's AGENTS.md and change it into this one, importing from src/kit.ts.
Keep to the main play and its score: leave out title, end and pause screens, menus and best scores.
Set it in the world from src/worlds.ts that fits the original, and keep its feel.
If it is not a game, first work out from its own code (its routes, its main screen, what its data is) what the app is for and the few things at its centre, the things a person works with in it.
Then write a short plan headed "What this app is in VR": what each of those things becomes in the space around the person, and what the person does with it with their hands. Then build it without waiting.
Build those things as things in the space around the person, never as pictures of the app's pages: for example a sky app's stars in the sky all around, a lesson's call stack as frames that stack up in front of you, a map's land at your feet.
Never make cards or a row of panels the main layout. Text appears only to show a thing's details, when the person points at the thing or presses it.
Never show a keyboard of letters on screen: the person points and presses. Where the app needs typed words, name that among what this version leaves out.
Make this VR version look and feel like this app alone, in its own colours, pictures, shapes and motion, never like a layout shared with other apps.
If it is not a game, build it in setupScene(world, colour) from src/kit.ts with the app's own page colour, never on the island and never in a world from src/worlds.ts, however well one fits its theme.
Build that use, working, with the app's own content. Its landing or marketing page is not its use, unless the app is nothing else.
Keep every part of that use: each way it finds things (a search, a filter) and each choice it asks for gets a way that works with a hand in VR, or is named among what this version leaves out.
If it is not a game, let the person reach every one of the app's things, never only the first few.
Draw every word in the scene, titles, labels, details and footnotes alike, no smaller than TYPE.words from src/kit.ts, which is sized to be read 1.6 m away; make words larger when they stand farther than that.
Where words do not fit, show fewer or page them; never shrink them.
Give every press its words, saying what it does; never a press without words, or a symbol alone.
What cannot come with it (a server, other people, an account), carry with the app's own sample content where it has some, and say so; never drop it silently.
If it is not a game, let its own look (its colours, pictures and effects) dress those things; when its use is itself a view (a sky, a map, a model), bring that view as 'A view from another app' in this project's AGENTS.md says.
Read every input through src/play-input.ts.
Copy pictures and sounds you need from the original into public/ and name them in src/assets.ts.
Its welcome card and page title already hold this project's name; keep it, and put one line on what to do on the card.
If it is not a game, put what of the app this version does not carry yet, or that it carries all of it, in a small panel the person opens with a press labelled "Not Here Yet", never in the main view; never call anything in it a stand-in or a demo.
If it is not a game, rewrite the card's laptop line (laptop-help in public/ui/welcome.uikitml) to say only what a mouse and keyboard really do in it: the starter's game keys do nothing there unless the app reads them.
Keep addParts(world) as the last call of the setup in src/index.ts, as every example at the end of AGENTS.md has it, so later tasks can each add to this app in a file of their own in src/parts/ without touching yours.
Run npx tsc --noEmit and fix every error it reports before you finish.
End your turn with a few lines for the person: what the app is for, what this VR version is, what of it comes from the app's sample content, and what it leaves out and why.
